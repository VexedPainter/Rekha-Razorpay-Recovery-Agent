 1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS, IN PLAIN ENGLISH
================================================================================

 THE PROBLEM
 -----------
 When someone tries to pay a business online, the payment sometimes fails. Their
 OTP times out. The bank has a glitch. They close the tab. The card expires.

 The business just loses that money. Most never chase it.

 Some of those failures ARE recoverable - the customer wanted to buy, something
 technical got in the way. If you send them a fresh payment link, many will pay.

 An AI is genuinely good at reading a failed payment and judging "this one is
 worth chasing, and here's how" versus "this one is dead, don't bother."

 So the obvious product is: an AI agent that reads your failed payments and
 chases the recoverable ones automatically.


 THE PROBLEM WITH THAT PRODUCT
 -----------------------------
 To do it, the AI needs your payment system's API keys.

 That means an AI - a thing that is non-deterministic, that can be tricked by
 text it reads, that cannot be audited after the fact - is holding the keys to
 your money.

 Ask yourself: would you hand an AI agent your Razorpay keys and let it move
 money unsupervised? Nobody sensible would.


 WHAT WE BUILT
 -------------
 We built the AI agent AND the thing that makes it safe to run.

 The AI can look at failed payments and PROPOSE recoveries. That is all it can
 do. It cannot execute anything.

 Every proposal goes through a separate layer - a "control plane" - that is
 ordinary deterministic code, no AI anywhere in it. That layer checks:

   - Is this action even allowed? (the merchant signed a "mandate" saying what
     the agent may do - e.g. "you may send payment links, you may NOT issue
     refunds")
   - Is this amount within the per-action limit?
   - Would this push us over the daily budget across ALL actions?
   - Is it big enough that a human must approve it first?
   - Have we already done this exact thing? (so a retry can't charge twice)

 Only if all of that passes does the action reach Razorpay.

 Then - and this is the part nobody else will have - after the money moves, the
 system checks Razorpay's own settlement records and asks:

   "Did the money that ACTUALLY moved match what we authorized?"

 If money moved that we never approved, it finds it.


 THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSION
 ------------------------
 An AI agent that recovers failed payments, wrapped in a deterministic control
 layer that means the AI can be wrong, tricked, or hostile and your money is
 still safe - and afterwards we prove from the bank's own records that the money
 that moved is the money we approved.


 THE SENTENCE THAT WINS THE ROOM
 -------------------------------
 "A compromised, jailbroken, or simply mistaken agent changes WHICH recoveries
  are attempted. It cannot change the mandate, the limits, the approval
  requirement, or the evidence."

 And critically: that is not a promise about how good our prompt is. It is
 enforced by code. There is a test that reads the source of the AI layer and
 FAILS THE BUILD if the AI code ever imports anything capable of approving,
 executing, or recording. The AI is structurally incapable of it.

================================================================================
 6. THE FIVE CONCEPTS YOU MUST BE ABLE TO EXPLAIN
================================================================================

 The panel grades "walk through your architecture". This is where people fail.
 Learn these five. Everything else you can look up.


 1. MANDATE vs POLICY - two documents, two different people
    The MANDATE is the merchant's authorisation. Their number. "Payment links
    yes, refunds no, max 5,000 per action, max 50,000 per day, human approval
    above 2,500."
    The POLICY is the operator's safety net, independent of the merchant. Hard
    denies at 10,000, refunds blocked outright, no customer messaging 9pm-9am.
    BOTH must pass. They answer to different people, so they're separate files.

 2. WHY THE AI CAN'T CHEAT
    The AI lives in one folder: recovery/. There's a test that reads the source
    code of every file in that folder and fails the build if any of them import
    the code that approves, executes, or records anything. The AI physically
    cannot reach those. It can only produce a proposal and hand it over.

 3. WHY MONEY IS AN INTEGER
    Money is stored as whole paise, never as a decimal number. Two reasons:
    Razorpay works in paise, so storing rupees invites an off-by-100 error in the
    worst possible place. And a limit check is a comparison - in computer
    floating-point arithmetic 0.1 + 0.2 is very slightly MORE than 0.3, and when
    you're adding up a day's spending that error drifts in the direction of
    letting the agent spend more than allowed. Integers make it exact.

 4. WHY "PENDING" AND "UNVERIFIABLE" ARE SEPARATE FROM "MATCHED"
    When there's no settlement data, the system says UNVERIFIABLE - not
    "matched". Absence of evidence is not agreement. If it said "matched" when it
    simply hadn't looked, every other verdict it gives would be worthless.

 5. WHY THE 10 LEGITIMATE PAYMENTS MATTER
    A system that blocks EVERYTHING scores a perfect 100% block rate. So "we
    blocked 6 of 6 attacks" means nothing on its own. The ten legitimate
    payments, all correctly allowed, are what makes the block rate meaningful.
    That's why the report shows both numbers together.


================================================================================
 7. QUESTIONS THEY WILL ASK - AND YOUR ANSWERS
================================================================================

 Q: "Why isn't the AI making the authorisation decision?"
 A: Because an AI is non-deterministic and promptable. Anything that decides
    whether money moves has to be a pure function of the proposal, the mandate,
    the policy and the ledger - so it's replayable and provable afterwards. The
    AI decides what to ATTEMPT. It never decides what's PERMITTED.

 Q: "What if someone jailbreaks your agent?"
 A: Run scenario 1. The AI does get jailbroken - it proposes the refund. The
    mandate refuses it. That's the whole design: I assume the AI will be
    compromised and make it not matter.

 Q: "How do you know the money actually moved correctly?"
 A: Three-way reconciliation. Our log, Razorpay's webhooks, Razorpay's settlement
    report. And I can detect money that settled with no authorisation at all -
    which is only findable from a record I don't author.

 Q: "Why not just use per-transaction limits?"
 A: Run the fan-out demo. Forty payments, each individually authorised, together
    blowing the budget by 60%. Per-transaction limits don't bound an agent.

 Q: "Is the settlement verification real?"
 A: The logic is real and tested against all four mismatch classes. The DATA is
    fixture-backed, because a fresh Razorpay test account returns zero
    settlements - settlement is a real banking event on a real cycle. The live
    code path is written, read-only, and currently returns nothing. It's in the
    README limitations section.
    -> VOLUNTEER THIS. Do not wait to be caught.

 Q: "Did you write all this yourself?"
 A: Be honest: I built the underlying control plane over about five weeks as a
    general AI-agent safety project - contracts, policy, approvals, the ledger.
    It was already L3 conformant with a written spec. I then retargeted it to
    payments, and built the financial domain, the Razorpay integration, the AI
    layer, webhooks and settlement verification on top. ADR 0028 documents
    exactly what was reused, what was deleted, and why.
    That's a STRONGER answer than "I built it in a weekend." Reusing your own
    tested infrastructure is what engineers do.

 Q: "What's not finished?"
 A: Have this ready:
    - Settlement data is fixture-backed (above)
    - Webhooks are replayed and signed by my own script, so verification is real
      but authenticity is asserted by me rather than Razorpay
    - No live Razorpay run yet, though the code path is wired
    - Razorpay's live payload shapes come from documentation, not observation

 Q: "What was the hardest bug?"
 A: The ledger was timestamping events with the real clock while the spending-
    window logic compared them against an injectable test clock. Two time sources
    for one comparison makes the window unsound, and it was untestable except by
    rewriting timestamps afterwards - which breaks the hash chain. There was
    already a test doing exactly that workaround.

 Q: "Why Gemini and not Claude?"
 A: The provider is behind an interface - Gemini, Groq, Anthropic, or recorded
    fixtures, selected by whichever key is present, free ones first. Nothing on
    the authorisation path depends on which model it is, and being able to say
    that is worth more than the model choice.

 IF YOU DON'T KNOW SOMETHING: say "I don't know, let me look" and open the file.
 That reads as competence. Guessing does not.
